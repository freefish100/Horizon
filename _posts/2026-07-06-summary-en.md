## [Organic Maps faces governance crisis, spawns CoMaps fork](https://organicmaps.app/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Community discussion reveals that the open-source map app Organic Maps has a fork called CoMaps, created due to governance concerns and allegations of adding ads and proprietary code. CoMaps is actively adding features like CarPlay support. This highlights the critical role of governance in open-source projects and demonstrates how conflicts can lead to community splits and forks. Users seeking a truly open-source and privacy-focused map app may migrate to CoMaps. Organic Maps is based on OpenStreetMap data and created by former MapsWithMe founders. According to comments, Organic Maps allegedly added ads, turned part of its code proprietary, and misappropriated donations, while CoMaps is actively developed with a focus on remaining fully open-source.

**Background**: Organic Maps is a privacy-focused offline map app that uses crowd-sourced data from OpenStreetMap. In open-source projects, governance models define decision-making processes and community trust; when governance fails, forks often emerge as alternatives. The CoMaps fork was created about a year ago amid growing dissatisfaction with Organic Maps' direction.

## [China Plans to Reduce SCI Publication Incentives to Prevent Tech Leaks](https://www.ft.com/content/64a811f1-b132-4211-8a8c-2252cf964039?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Chinese policymakers are discussing reducing incentives for researchers to publish in SCI-indexed international journals, moving to lower the weight of SCI papers in academic promotions and tenure decisions due to national security concerns over technology leakage. This shift could fundamentally change China's research evaluation system, potentially reducing the global influence of Chinese science and altering international collaboration patterns. It also signals growing tension between open scientific exchange and national security. Since August 2023, China has tightened regulation on foreign academic publishing, and the National Natural Science Foundation now requires at least 20% of representative papers from funded projects to be published in Chinese-language journals. One researcher claims he has stopped submitting to foreign journals due to vague and increasingly strict security reviews.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jul 6, 01:03

**Background**: The Science Citation Index (SCI) is a widely used citation database that tracks citations from thousands of scientific journals. In China, SCI publication has long been a key metric for academic evaluation, heavily influencing funding, promotions, and tenure. However, concerns about technology leakage via academic papers have prompted officials to reconsider this policy, as sensitive research details may be exposed through the peer review process.
